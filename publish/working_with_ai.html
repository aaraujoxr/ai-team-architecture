Working
with AI

From prompts to context engineering.
Four problems anyone serious about AI has felt — and the system that falls out of taking them together.
Artur Ferreira de Araujo · 2026
01 / THE OPENING
Most people using AI seriously hit the same wall.
Stay in one long session and precision decays as the context fills with noise.
Split into separate sessions and the AI remembers none of what came before. Re-explaining each time is slow. Pasting yesterday's chat brings the bloat right back.
These two failure modes are what you swing between when you think in prompts. Neither side of the swing is solved by better prompts.
02 / THE REFRAME
Stop tuning prompts. Start engineering context.
A prompt is what you send into a single turn. Context engineering is managing what the AI sees — across turns and across sessions — deliberately.
The artifact you maintain is the context, not any individual prompt. Files hold that context in distilled form: the conclusions of yesterday's work, not the transcript of how it was reached.
What follows: four problems you've already felt, each pointing at a conclusion. Then the system that falls out when you take all four together.
Part 1
Four problems you've already felt.
Each one is a wall you hit on your own as soon as you try to do real work with AI. Each one points at a conclusion. Taken together, they describe a system.
03 / PROBLEM 1
Correction cost is asymmetric
Ambiguity caught before implementation costs a sentence. Caught after, it costs a debugging cycle that's hard to escape.
BeforeA sentence in conversation
"Should this handle the edge case where X happens?" gets answered in a beat. The implementation that follows is targeted at the right thing from the start.
AfterA debugging loop you can't escape
The AI's interpretation lives in its context. You correct one piece; it patches around the existing implementation. You explain more clearly; it produces another variant close to the wrong version. The context that was supposed to help is now anchoring the wrong premise — and every cycle burns tokens.
Conclusion 1
Align and plan before executing. Time up front isn't a delay before the real work — it's the cheapest place to surface what would otherwise become expensive.
04 / PROBLEM 2
The relevant context is mostly tacit
Writing a spec without the conversation gives you the original request in document form — same gaps, same unspoken assumptions.
WhyThe constraints that matter aren't written down
They're held by the person who knows the legacy system, who's seen this edge case before, who remembers why the previous approach was rejected. Not hidden — just unarticulated, often until the right question is asked.
HowAlignment is the conversation that surfaces it
The AI asks questions that pull tacit constraints into the open. You answer, and in answering you discover things about your own intent that weren't clear when you started. Edge cases come up. Trade-offs become visible. The conversation does real cognitive work — yours, not the AI's.
Conclusion 2
Align in conversation; capture in specs. The spec is the output of an important process, not the input to one.
05 / PROBLEM 3
Every session starts from zero
You did the alignment work well. You have a sharp spec. The next session opens and the AI doesn't remember any of it.
TrapTwo workarounds, both bad
Re-explaining the spec from scratch each session is slow and imprecise. Pasting yesterday's chat back in skips the re-explanation but brings the bloat right back.
Way outFiles hold the digested form of what came before
The spec, the resolved questions, the assumptions surfaced during alignment, the conclusions of yesterday's session. Short relative to the conversations that produced them, because they hold conclusions rather than reasoning paths. They can be loaded selectively, so each session's context contains exactly what's relevant — and not what isn't.
Conclusion 3
Context must persist through files, not chat history. The unit of memory across sessions is the digested artifact, not the transcript.
06 / PROBLEM 4
The context window is the AI's cognitive mode — and it only has one
More context isn't always better. Sometimes it's what makes the AI worse.
ExampleThe author cannot be the reviewer
The session that did the work has, by definition, the context that produced the work. Every assumption is already loaded. The missing case won't surface — because the missing case isn't in the context.
WhyWhatever's in the context shapes how it reasons
A human reviewer can — with effort — hold their own draft at arm's length. An AI cannot. There is no other gear. Cold review requires a different session with a different context: one that doesn't include the reasoning that produced the artifact under review.
MoreThe same principle is wider than review
Research vs. development. Planning vs. execution. Each mode is sharper when its context is built for it — uncluttered by what doesn't help it.
Conclusion 4
Separate roles for different cognitive modes. Not bureaucracy — the only way to get cold eyes once you're working with AI.
07 / TOGETHER
Putting the four together
These aren't independent recommendations. They sit on top of each other — and a shape falls out.
01

02
Align in conversation; capture in specs.
03
Persist context in files.
04
Separate roles for different cognitive modes.
The shape
Work happens in defined sessions. Each session starts with alignment and ends with files. The files become the next session's starting point. When work crosses cognitive modes, it crosses through files — not through conversations.
Part 2
The system that falls out.
Nothing in it is invented. Every piece follows from one of the four problems. Naming the pieces is what comes next.
08 / FILES
Files are the substrate, not records
Not a record of what the AI produced. The working medium the AI thinks through.
01Role definitions
Describe a cognitive mode — what this role thinks about, what it doesn't see, what its outputs look like.
02Knowledge files
Patterns, conventions, and recurring questions that any session of a given kind should benefit from. General and reusable. They accumulate over time and compound across projects, shaping how a role operates.
03Alignment artifacts
Specs, design docs, decision records. The digested output of an alignment conversation about a particular task. Work-specific — they come and go with the work they document.
A session begins by reading the relevant files. It ends by updating them. The files are what carries forward — not the chat history of how they came to be.
09 / SESSIONS
Sessions have shape: Align → Work → Capture
Three beats. The third is the one most often skipped — and it's the most consequential.
Align
Surface tacit knowledge
Find the questions, agree on the approach. The conversation that pulls constraints into the open.
Work
Do the substantive thing
Refine a plan, implement against a spec, explore a question, review an artifact. Often a separate session from alignment, with a file between them.
Capture
Write back what's new
The spec, the knowledge note, the decision record, the updated state. The beat that makes the next session start further along — instead of from zero.
A session that doesn't capture is a session that has to be partly re-done.
10 / LAYERS
Two layers — the team layer is the unlock
Both are necessary. People come to AI with project-level habits and skip the team layer entirely. That's the layer where investment compounds most steeply.
Project layer
What this thing is
Codebase, data, constraints specific to this domain, conventions in this project. The layer people reach for first — that's where the actual work happens.
Compounds within a project.
Team layer
How you and your team work
Analytical defaults, writing register, who reviews what, feedback protocol, the patterns you apply across all your work, what you've learned about how AI helps you best.
Compounds across your working life.
Why it's the unlock
Project docs don't naturally have a home for "how you work." Without the team layer, the AI can read your code but doesn't know how you reason, what your defaults are, when to ask vs. proceed. One hour spent sharpening the team context affects every future session, every project, every role.
11 / ROLES
Role separation as context specialization
A minimum split: one role that plans and aligns, one that executes, one that reviews. Each a separate session, with its own context.
01Separation is specialization, not just adversarial
A planning context can hold questions, alternatives, trade-offs. An execution context can hold the decided plan and the code. A review context can hold the artifact and the criteria, without the reasoning that produced it. Each role's context is sharper because it isn't asked to do every job at once.
02Separation is about access, not just instructions
Telling a single AI session "now act as a reviewer" doesn't get you cold review — the context still contains the implementation reasoning. Cold review requires a different session loaded with a different set of files. Small but consequential, and easy to underestimate.
Part 3
What changes in how you work.
Step back from the specific problems. With the system in place, the shape of the change is bigger than any single piece.
12 / CHANGES
Four changes
The version of "AI augments you" that actually happens in practice.
01Your time shifts to where your contribution matters
The expensive part — debugging-and-steering after AI has committed to the wrong interpretation — largely disappears. The cheap, high-leverage part — alignment, where your judgment matters — gets the most attention.
02The work compounds
A team context that captures how you think becomes more useful with every refinement, and carries across every project. Knowledge files accumulate. The substrate gets denser. Sessions start further along. Alignment conversations get shorter because the recurring questions are already answered.
03You stop fighting the AI's grain
You're not asking it to context-switch, review its own work, infer tacit knowledge from a sparse request, or carry memory without a substrate. Each is something AI is structurally bad at. Friction drops because the system matches how AI actually works.
04You can delegate more confidently
Role separation gives you structural cold eyes. AI can execute against a clear spec knowing a separately-contexted review will catch what slipped through. Trust is grounded in the structure of the system, not in hoping AI got it right.
13 / EXTENSIONS
Once it's in place, more becomes composable
Not new ideas — continuations. The substrate makes the rest possible. Without sessions and files and clear roles, none of this layering works.
01A ledger of where work stands
A small additional file that tracks what's done, what's blocked, what's next, what was decided and why. Turns the substrate from passive storage into evolving state. A new session reads the ledger first and orients in seconds.
02Hygiene scripts
Once your work runs on a substrate with a clear shape, agents can help maintain it: checking the ledger is consistent, distilling scattered notes, verifying role definitions match expectations.
03Version control integration
A session has natural boundaries — alignment artifact, implementation, capture — that map closely to commits and PRs. A workstream maps to a branch. Alignment artifacts make natural PR descriptions.
04Project and ticket integration
Workstreams correspond to tickets. The ledger is where the real status of work lives — more accurate than the ticket tool, because it's updated by the work itself. Tying the two together means the project board reflects reality.
14 / CLOSE
The shift isn't being better at prompting. It's doing the structural work upfront.
Surface what you know. Persist it in files. Separate cognitive modes. So the AI has somewhere to think — and so do you.
Every piece of the system follows from a problem you've already felt. Nothing is invented; it's recognized. The argument is that the recognition is enough, once the problems are named.
